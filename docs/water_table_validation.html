Held-out validation of handily depth-to-water estimates

A depth-banded accuracy check of handily, a CONUS water-table model, against two published national reference products, Ma and Janssen V2, on identical wells, footprint, and metrics. handily is a version 0.1 beta. It is built from observation-based features and naive physical priors and does not take the output of other groundwater models as input; the two reference products are themselves fitted model outputs. Scoring uses GWX unconfined wells across three regions where handily has been rendered to 10 m. handily is held out of its own training wells; the reference products cannot be, so the comparison is conservative for handily.

1 · handily

What handily is, and what it is compared against

handily estimates depth to the unconfined water table (positive down, in meters) across the contiguous United States, rendered here as a 10 m grid. It is assembled from observation-based features and naive physical priors: terrain and height-above-nearest-drainage, flow-accumulation drainage structure, climate and recharge covariates, and water-level observations. handily development is focused on data-leakage-free experimentation at this early stage: other model outputs (e.g., ParFlow, CONUS MODFLOW) may be helpful later, but the current effort is to make the model as good as possible using only terrain, remote sensing, climate proxies, and well data. Later experiments with data assimilation and pre-training may consume the output of other groundwater models as input. The two reference products used here are, by contrast, fitted statistical models of well observations: Ma is a random forest (Ma et al., 2026, doi:10.1038/s43247-025-03094-3) and Janssen V2 is a gradient-boosted model (Janssen et al., 2024, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2025.104926).

This report is an early baseline for a version 0.1 beta. It has discontinuities, gaps, and places where the inference doesn't make sense, but sharing it early to get feedback is important. This doc reports where the current estimates are and are not useful, on the same wells and metrics for all three products.

2 · approach

What this validation does

The three depth-to-water predictors are sampled at the same well locations and compared on identical wells and metrics. The design choices below control for leakage and keep the comparison like-for-like.

Held-out scoring — the leakage control that matters most

The handily grid you are sampling is final-model inference: the production model was trained on every available well and then rendered everywhere. Sampling that grid at a well the model trained on would measure memorization, not prediction. So handily is scored only on wells it never saw: any GWX well whose location matches a training well (to the meter, on the EPSG:5070 grid) is dropped via the model's out-of-fold record before a single metric is computed. This makes handily strictly conservative here.

An asymmetry to keep in mind: handily can be held out because its training wells are known. Ma's and Janssen's training wells are not known, so they cannot be held out and retain whatever memorization advantage they carry. Ma is trained on a random cell split and its apparent error collapses to a fraction of its true error where its training network is dense, notably on NWIS and NGWMN monitoring wells. Those two sources are therefore excluded from every number in this report. Net effect: the reference products are, if anything, scored on easier terms than handily.

Raster sampling across three coordinate systems

Each predictor is sampled at the well's longitude/latitude after reprojecting that point into the raster's own CRS — handily on the CONUS Albers 10 m grid, Ma on its Lambert conformal grid, Janssen on geographic degrees. No raster is resampled to a common grid before sampling, which would blur it; the point moves to the pixel instead. A well counts only where all three predictors return a finite value (the common footprint), so every row of every table compares like with like on identical wells.

Which wells, and how they are grouped

  • Unconfined only. Confined and likely-confined wells measure a pressure surface, not the water table, and are dropped. Only unconfined and unconfined_marginal wells enter the metrics.
  • Depth bands. Every metric is stratified by observed depth — 0–2 / 2–5 / 5–10 / 10–30 / 30+ m — because a single headline hides the one fact that governs everything: shallow-terrain priors are strong near the surface and saturate with depth.
  • Setting. Wells are split into valley vs. upland by distance to a mapped stream, so a shallow-precision claim can be read where it is actually used.

The metrics

Convention. Residual = predicted − observed; a negative residual means the estimate is shallower than observed. Bias and median residual are shown together: when the mean bias is much larger than the median residual, the error is a skewed tail rather than a uniform offset. In each depth band, the best value in each metric column is shown in bold green — lowest for the error metrics, closest to zero for bias and median residual.

4 · results: new mexico

New Mexico — 77 basins

New Mexico has a deep depth distribution: 42% of the unconfined wells are 30 m or deeper, a regime no terrain-following estimate reaches. That deep fraction dominates the pooled MAD, which is close for handily and Ma (8.98 and 8.84). In the depth bands, handily has the lower MAD below 10 m — roughly half Ma's error at 2–5 m — and Ma has the lower MAD in the 10–30 m band and the 30+ m tail.

5 · results: upper colorado

Upper Colorado — 29 basins

The depth distribution here is shallower: only 7% of wells are 30+ m. handily has the lower pooled MAD (2.63 vs Ma 3.91) and the lower MAD in the 0–2, 2–5, and 5–10 m bands. Ma again has the lower MAD in the 10–30 m band and the deep tail, the same depth crossover seen in the other regions; with few deep wells present it does not change the pooled result.

6 · results: nevada

Nevada — 72 basins

Nevada is Great-Basin closed-basin terrain. Ma has the lower pooled MAD (7.00 vs handily 7.98). The difference is concentrated in the 10–30 m band, where handily is 11.0 and Ma is 6.3; below 10 m handily has the lower MAD in each band (2–5 m: 1.91 vs Ma 4.09), and in the 30+ m tail Janssen V2 has the lowest MAD of the three. The mid-depth, non-near-stream regime is where the terrain-based estimate is weakest.

7 · shallow water table

The shallow water table

handily is intended for the shallow, near-stream water table. Treating a depth threshold as a shallow/not-shallow classification, handily and Ma show a consistent trade: handily has higher recall and lower precision than Ma. At the <5 m threshold in New Mexico, handily recalls 81% of shallow wells at 0.41 precision; Ma recalls 55% at 0.64 precision. The preferred product depends on the relative cost of a missed shallow well versus a false shallow call.

Read with the counts. Precision/recall are computed on the valley setting (upland wells number in the low hundreds and don't support a stable rate). n obs is how many wells are truly shallower than the threshold; n pred is how many each predictor calls shallow — handily's larger n pred is the visible source of its higher recall and lower precision.

8 · summary

Summary

The same depth-dependent pattern holds in all three regions. handily has the lower MAD in every depth band shallower than 10 m, generally by roughly a factor of two relative to Ma. Ma has the lower MAD in the 10–30 m band in every region. Limits of these estimates

  • The 30+ m regime is not resolved by any of the three products. handily's deep error appears as a large negative bias — estimates shallower than observed — which the MAD understates and the RMSE reflects. A deep regional water-table datum is a physical input the current features do not carry.
  • The 10–30 m band is where handily is weakest relative to Ma, most visibly in the Nevada closed basins. This mid-depth, non-near-stream regime, rather than the deep tail, accounts for the pooled difference there.
  • The comparison is conservative for handily. handily is held out of its training wells; the reference products are not, and Ma's highest-leakage sources are excluded. The shallow-band results hold under those terms.

In summary, handily is most useful for the shallow, near-stream water table, where it has the lower depth-banded error in all three regions. It is not a deep regional-aquifer model, and this validation does not evaluate it as one.
